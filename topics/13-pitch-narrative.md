# Pitch narrative structure
{: .no_toc }

*~8 min read*

## Why it matters

You will explain the company hundreds of times: to a user, a recruit, a professor, an angel. A pitch is not a deck and not a performance. It is a short sequence of sentences that lets a stranger understand what you do, why it matters now, and what you want from them. If you cannot say it in two minutes, you do not yet understand it — and no slide design will hide that.

The content of the pitch is the work from earlier pages. If the sentences feel empty, the fix is usually more user conversations, not more slides.

## Core concepts

- **Start with what you do, in words a non-founder repeats.** "We help [specific person] [do the job] without [the painful workaround]." Not a category ("we're a platform") and not a technology ("we're an AI-powered solution"). If your parent would not get the first sentence, it isn't done. This is the same sentence as the [wedge](../02-idea-wedge/).
- **Then the proof you have met that person.** One concrete detail from a real conversation or a real usage event beats a market-size paragraph. "Three club treasurers reconciled last month's dues in our sheet" is a pitch. "The student-organizations market is $4B" is a dodge unless someone asked for size.
- **Why you, why now — one sentence each.** Why you can reach them. What changed so that the workaround is worse or the product is newly possible. Both must be checkable. "We're passionate" is not why you.
- **What you have done, in order of impressiveness to *this* listener.** A user cares what changes for them tomorrow. A hire cares what they will build and whether the founders tell the truth. An investor cares what you have done since you started: growth, usage, revenue, speed. Lead with the strongest true fact. Do not lead with the vision and hope they stay awake.
- **The ask is specific.** A user: try it on Thursday. A hire: a work session next week. An investor: a next meeting, or a check of a size you can say out loud. "We'd love your thoughts" wastes the only moment they were ready to do something.
- **Two lengths are enough.** Thirty seconds (what it is, who it's for) and two minutes (add insight, traction, ask). Ten-minute monologues create chances to say something they dislike. Let them pull more. Slides, if you use any, follow this order — they are not the narrative.
- **Clarity is the whole craft.** Delete jargon, acronyms, and "X for Y" analogies that require the listener to know X. Read it out loud to someone outside the team. Where they frown, rewrite. Where they say "oh, like ___," steal their words.

## What to do next

- [ ] Write the 30-second version on paper. Three sentences maximum: what it is, who it's for and why it's painful, what you want.
- [ ] Write the two-minute version as six beats: what it is, the painful story, why now, what you've done, why you, the ask. Time yourself. If you pass two minutes, cut a beat, don't talk faster.
- [ ] Say it to one person who is not a cofounder this week. Ask them to repeat what you do. Edit until their version matches yours.
- [ ] Make a one-page leave-behind only after the spoken version works: those same beats, almost no adjectives. A deck is optional; a muddy deck is worse than no deck.
- [ ] Keep a "proof" line updated weekly from the [metrics](../07-metrics-that-matter/) spreadsheet so the traction sentence never drifts into fiction.
- [ ] Practice the ask until it doesn't apologize. "We're exploring" is not an ask.

## Mental model

```
  30 seconds
  -----------
  1. We help [who] do [job].
  2. Today they [painful workaround].
  3. Ask: [specific next step].

  2 minutes adds, in this order
  ------------------------------
  4. One true story or number.
  5. Why now (a change, not a mood).
  6. Why this team reaches these users.
  then stop talking.

  listener            lead with
  ---------           ---------
  user                their job, this week
  recruit             the work and the truth about runway
  investor            what you have already done
```

Same company, same spine. Different first proof. Never a different fiction.

## Common failure modes

- **Opening with the market or the model.** You have about one sentence before they decide whether to listen. Use it on the job you do.
- **A vision paragraph with no fact.** Ambition without "here is what happened last week" is a class project. Include the small true number.
- **Burying the ask, or having five asks.** Pick one action. You can want intros *and* a pilot *and* a hire; you ask for the one this person can do.
- **Memorizing adjectives.** "Revolutionary, seamless, AI-native" is how listeners learn you don't have a sentence. Specifics are the style.
